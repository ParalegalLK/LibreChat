- Title: `License Agreements in Sri Lanka`

## Prompt

`is a license agreement pertaining to the use of an immovable property enforceable in a court in sri lanka`

## Answer

Based on the leading Sri Lankan legal textbook [The Law of Contracts Vol. 1](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation) by C. G. Weeramantry, a license agreement pertaining to the use of immovable property is generally not enforceable in a court in Sri Lanka unless it complies with the strict formal requirements set out in the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance (Cap. 70). The enforceability depends on the nature of the rights granted and the form of the agreement.

### Key Points from the Textbook

1. Section 2 of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance requires that any contract or agreement for the sale, transfer, assignment, mortgage, or for establishing any security, interest, or encumbrance affecting land or other immovable property must be in writing, signed in the presence of a licensed notary public and two or more witnesses, and attested ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), p. 164).

2. Leases and licences: The law distinguishes between leases, which grant exclusive possession, and licences, which grant only a right to use without exclusive possession. The test is whether the agreement gives exclusive possession. If so, it is likely to be treated as a lease and thus subject to the formalities of section 2 ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), p. 212).

3. Licences not amounting to an interest in land: If the agreement is a mere licence, meaning it does not grant exclusive possession or any interest in the land, it may not fall within the scope of section 2. However, the courts have held that even informal leases or licences can have some legal consequences, such as supporting a claim for compensation for use and occupation, but not for enforcement of the agreement itself as a contract creating an interest in land ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), pp. 167, 177).

> "There would be no objection however to the lease being used for purely evidentiary purposes as for example to establish leave and licence and to negative any suggestion of trespass."

> "An action for use and occupation may be maintained even though there is no notarial lease relating to the land."

4. Enforceability of informal agreements: Agreements that do not comply with the notarial requirements are of no force or avail in law for the purpose of creating or transferring an interest in immovable property. However, such agreements may be used as evidence for limited purposes, for example to show the nature of occupation or to support a claim for compensation, but cannot be enforced as contracts creating rights in land ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), pp. 172-173).

5. Exception for short-term leases: Leases at will or for a period not exceeding one month are exempt from the notarial requirement ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), p. 166).

6. Licences distinguished from leases: The Supreme Court has held that a permit or licence that does not grant exclusive possession is not a lease and does not require notarial execution ([chapter-08-form.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=books%2Fthe-law-of-contracts-vol-01%2Fchapter-08-form.md&name=The%20Law%20of%20Contracts%20Vol.%201&area=litigation), p. 212, citing `Wijesuriya v. Attorney-General`).

### Practical Effect

- If the license agreement is, in substance, a lease or grants an interest in land, for example exclusive possession for a term, it must be notarially executed to be enforceable in court.
- If it is a mere licence, with no exclusive possession and no interest in land, it may be enforceable as a contract, but only to the extent that it does not purport to create or transfer an interest in immovable property. Remedies may be limited to damages or compensation for use and occupation, not specific performance or recognition of a proprietary right.

### Corporate Context

The Companies Act No. 7 of 2007 recognizes licences as binding obligations in respect of secured property, but this is within the context of corporate security instruments and does not override the requirements of the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance for interests in immovable property ([companies-act-no-7-of-2007.md](https://docs.desaram.ai/document.html/?view=document&path=corporate%2Facts%2Fcompanies-act-no-7-of-2007.md&name=acts-companies-act-no-7-of-2007&area=corporate), para 4(d)).

### Conclusion

A license agreement relating to the use of immovable property is only enforceable in a Sri Lankan court if it does not purport to create an interest in land and does not grant exclusive possession. If it does, it must comply with the Prevention of Frauds Ordinance, meaning it must be notarially executed and attested. Otherwise, it is not enforceable as a contract creating rights in land, though it may have limited evidentiary or compensatory effect.
